Secure CAN Monitor · Raspberry Pi 3 · OP-TEE

Video slide, one minute, recorded from a real run on the two Pis on 25 November 2026. It plays in a loop while this slide is presented; in a PowerPoint or PDF download only the still picture shows, so keep the deck link for the video. Captions explain it as it runs. The top window is the sender, the car: every packet looks normal and green, and it does not mark its attacks. The bottom window is the receiver: Linux only carries the frames, and the Secure World on core 1 judges every one and colors it by its verdict. Green is valid. Red is a tampered packet (the signature no longer matches), orange a replay (an old packet again), light blue a duplicate, purple a conflict (same counter, other data), pink a wrong CAN id, yellow a counter gap. An attack comes every 2 to 3 seconds, and all six types appear. The banner at the top of the receiver shows the last detection, the counters show how many of each, and the bottom line shows the Secure World's own counters since boot. One command starts the demo live: bash scripts/demo_live_stream.sh. To use your own recording, drop an .mp4 of 20 MB or less onto the picture in the editor.
