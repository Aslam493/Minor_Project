Secure CAN Monitor · Raspberry Pi 3 · OP-TEE

Left: the state of the art and how we go further. AUTOSAR SecOC uses counters against replay; we use the same idea, but the check runs in the Secure World. TEECheck, at RTNS 2020, vets frames at the sender with a TEE, which is prevention; our monitor detects at the receiver and still works when Linux is compromised. iCORE, at SAC 2019, keeps a core in the Secure World from boot for monitoring; our core 1 uses this idea to judge CAN traffic. EmLog, published at WISTP 2018, is tamper-resistant logging in a TEE and is the model for our secure log. Right: the five promises from the midterm, in its own words. HMAC and latency are done; storing packets in flash is designed but not built; rate and flood is partly done, because every dropped frame is counted but there is no flood alarm; and the end-to-end demo with real measurements is done. The gap: in the work we surveyed, none detects CAN attacks from OP-TEE when Linux itself is compromised.
