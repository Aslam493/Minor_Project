Secure CAN Monitor · Raspberry Pi 3 · OP-TEE

Left: the three ways trusted CAN handling is done today, as shown at midterm. Checking in Linux is simple but trusts the operating system; an external HSM is isolated but adds hardware. Ours uses TrustZone on the same chip with a dedicated core. Right: the five items promised at midterm, in the midterm's own words. Three are done. Rate and flood detection is partly done: every dropped frame is counted exactly, but there is no separate flood alarm. Storing validated packets in flash is not built; it is designed as a forward-secure log and is the first item of future work. The end-to-end result: 31 of 32 test cases passed on the first run; the one failure, the forwarder not starting at boot (E2b), was fixed and re-tested, and one new limit was found after the fix (E2c).
