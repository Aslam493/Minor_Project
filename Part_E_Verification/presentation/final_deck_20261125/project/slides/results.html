All 32 original cases ran on the real two-Pi CAN-FD bus: 31 passed the first time. The bars show the first run: boot and setup 4 of 4, normal traffic 3 of 3, attacks 10 of 10, load and loss 12 of 12, restart and reboot 2 of 3. The one failure, E2b, was that the forwarder did not start by itself at boot. It was fixed with a new image and re-tested, and now passes (3 of 3). After the fix, one new limit appeared, E2c: controlling the service over ssh does not return; the workaround is documented and the fix is queued for the next image. Twelve limits are documented, none hidden. Right: packet loss under load. The three bars are totals over 3 runs each: the 1,004-frame burst lost nothing; steady 943 frames per second dropped 9,203 of 42,000; a flood of 3,587 frames per second dropped 80,040 of 105,000. Every drop is counted: sent equals judged plus each counted loss, and the queue drops equal the gap the Secure World sees. Nothing is lost up to 640 frames per second and bursts of about 2,490 frames. All 18 load runs of the loss series matched exactly; 12 of them dropped frames, and in those 12 the counted drops equal the Secure World's gap; 278,204 frames were accounted. Bottom: trust proofs checked on the board on 25 November: Linux sees only cores 0, 2 and 3 while the Secure World loop counter on core 1 grows by about 39.5 million per second; the Secure World memory is reserved in the Linux memory map; and the forwarder binary and the monitor client contain none of the three keys. The keys are prototype keys built into the image, and the monitor detects but does not block, because the Pi 3 has no TZASC.
